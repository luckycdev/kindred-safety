About

Nobody should have to walk home alone.

Kindred started as an argument about a five-minute walk — and turned into a product about the gap between “something feels wrong” and “someone knows.”

Most of the frightening moments in an ordinary life are short, ambiguous, and close to home. A stairwell that's darker than usual. A car that slows down twice. A shift that ends after the last bus. In almost every one of those moments nothing bad happens — and in the handful where something does, the person it happens to is the one least able to explain where they are.

Phones already solve this, technically. They have a camera, a GPS radio, a data connection and a speaker loud enough to turn heads. What they don't have is a single control that uses all four at once, that works with the screen locked, and that reaches exactly the people who would drop everything — and nobody else.

That control is the entire product. Everything else in Kindred exists to make one button trustworthy.

What we build

A personal security system that lives in your pocket. Live video, live location, an alert that reaches your circle in parallel rather than one call at a time, a siren for when attention is the point, and an encrypted archive so that afterwards there is a record and it belongs to you.

What we won't build

  • A public feed. There is no discovery, no follower count, no way for a stranger to find your stream.
  • A surveillance tool. A viewer can never start your camera. Consent runs one way: outward from the person holding the phone.
  • An ad business. The subscription is the business model. There are no ad SDKs, no analytics brokers and no data sales — which is also why there's no free tier.
  • A false promise. Kindred is not an emergency service and never says it is. It alerts people who love you. Professionals are a phone call we'll never replace.

The promises

  • Your clips are encrypted before they leave your device with a key we do not hold.
  • Deleting a clip deletes it — from the archive, from the backups, within 72 hours.
  • We publish every law-enforcement request we receive, in aggregate, twice a year.
  • If the company is ever sold, subscribers get 90 days' notice and a full export before anything changes.

Who we are

A small team spread across three time zones: four engineers, one designer, one person who answers every support email personally. We've all been the one waiting up, and we've all been the one walking.
